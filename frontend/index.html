    How the radar decides

    Calm water reflects the radar pulse away from the satellite and shows up dark. On every pass we map that water at 20 m, look for a continuous water path from each community's landing to Tefé, and measure the narrowest point of that path.

    A community counts as isolated only when two passes in a row show no water route. Places that never show a route the radar can resolve, and places away from any water, are left out of the counts.

    The radar sees water, not depth. A channel can still be there and be too shallow for boats, which is why routes narrower than 60 m are flagged as at risk. Check against river gauges and local reports before acting.
